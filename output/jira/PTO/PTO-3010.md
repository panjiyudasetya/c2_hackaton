h3. *Observation Report*

Each visit follows two steps: first its fact ship-to-ship transfers and visits complete ({{facts_completed_at}}), then its operational queue entry is picked up and marked done ({{updated_at}}). The gap between these moments matters and is consistently *9 to 12 hours*, regardless of when the operational queue row was created.

!image-20260910-045343.png|width=502,alt="image-20260910-045343.png"!

The timeline graph is the evidence for this. It puts both groups of visits on the same real clock, so you can see the actual waiting happen rather than just read numbers in a table:

* The top row's queue entry ({{c0ce50e1c224.VISIT}}, {{aa5e82a32502.VISIT}}, {{f6ed5f56282b.VISIT}}, and {{da94dd05a36e.VISIT}}) was created way back on Sep 8, long before its facts even finished on Sep 9 at 10:18. It might give the impression that the next fact operational transformation would be first in line, but it still waited until 19:46, a *9hour 29 minute* gap after facts completed.
* The bottom row's queue entry ({{b549663f6456.VISIT}}) was created right at 10:18, the moment facts finished, essentially no head start needed. Yet it waited even longer, until 21:49, a *11 hour 32 minute* gap.

The graph shows the opposite of what you’d expect, [~accountid:712020:139cdf21-5f28-404e-8f78-42a2f6d87ec7]: the row that became "ready" earliest didn’t finish sooner, and the row that started immediately didn’t finish faster. Both landed in the same window. This shows the wait depends not on when a row becomes ready but on the backlog: with the default setting of 500 items per run, a ready row sits until the queue reaches it.

In short, with the default 500-per-run pickup limit, expect roughly 9 to 12 hours of queueing delay between a visit's facts finishing and its operational queue actually showing as updated, and that delay is a function of backlog depth and batch size, not per-record processing time.

Therefore, [~accountid:712020:6e1b2474-0fae-4f80-8d72-c9b81c4d2ead] has increased the queue config to process 2,500 queues per run, which should make them available in MART sooner.
