# Penetration Testing Areas

1. **Kubernetes RBAC & Permissions**  
   Verify that users, service accounts, and workloads only have the permissions they need. Excessive Kubernetes permissions can allow attackers to access sensitive resources, escalate privileges, or take control of the cluster.
2. **IAM Roles for Service Accounts (IRSA)**  
   Review which AWS IAM roles/identities are assigned to Kubernetes workloads. Overly broad permissions can allow a compromised application to access AWS resources such as S3 buckets, Secrets Manager, databases, or even administrative AWS functions.
3. **Secrets Management**  
   Assess how secrets are stored and accessed across Kubernetes and AWS. Determine whether workloads, users, or roles can access credentials, API keys, certificates, or other sensitive information beyond what is required.
4. **Pod Security Configuration**  
   Review pod security settings to identify workloads that have elevated privileges, access to the host system, or permissions that could enable container escape or node compromise.
5. **Worker Node Security**  
   Evaluate the security posture of EKS worker nodes, including their AWS permissions, operating system hardening, and the potential impact if a node becomes compromised.
6. **Network Segmentation**  
   Assess whether workloads are properly isolated from one another. A compromised application should not be able to freely communicate with databases, internal services, or other sensitive components unless explicitly required.
7. **Software Supply Chain**  
   Examine container images and deployment pipelines for vulnerabilities, outdated software, untrusted sources, and inadequate controls around what can be deployed into the cluster.
8. **CI/CD Security**  
   Evaluate deployment pipelines and automation systems. Compromising a CI/CD platform often provides a direct path into production environments, cloud resources, and secrets stores.
9. **Cluster Administration & Access Management**  
   Review who has administrative access to Kubernetes and AWS. Ensure access is appropriately controlled, audited, and aligned with the principle of least privilege.
10. **AWS Privilege Escalation Paths**  
    Review IAM configurations for opportunities to gain additional AWS privileges through role assumption, policy modification, or other permission chains that could lead to full account compromise.
11. **Monitoring, Logging & Detection**  
    Determine whether malicious activities would be detected and investigated. Security controls should provide visibility into privilege escalation attempts, unauthorized access, and suspicious behavior.

## Key Questions to Answer During the Assessment

1. If an attacker compromises a pod, what can they access?
2. Can a compromised pod obtain AWS permissions beyond its intended scope?
3. Can an attacker access secrets, credentials, or sensitive data?
4. Can they move laterally to other workloads or environments?
5. Can they gain administrative control of Kubernetes or AWS?
6. Would the organization detect and respond to these activities?
7. What is the potential business impact if a single workload is compromised?
