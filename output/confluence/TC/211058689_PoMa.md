# PoMa

**Space:** TC  
**URL:** https://teqplaybv.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/TC/pages/211058689  

## Content

PoMa is a Core Service within Teqplay holding the relevant context mapping on ports and port attributes. It has been set up as the central information source for such information, and as such is the authoritative source for port information including terminals, berths, pilot boarding places and anchorages.

PoMa has a backend with an API, which is to be used by all applications within Teqplay to retrieve any port context data. In order to facilitate the management of the data, a visual front-end has been built on top of the backend. The front-end is only used by the Teqplay team to validate and add port context data into the system in an easy and visual way.

## 1. Data origin

Originally PoMa was set up as a combination of information sources received from port and fairway authorities, like port of Rotterdam, port of Amsterdam, North sea ports etc. combined with manually updated information, like manually drawn port borders for 3000+ ports. Such data was entered by the context mapping team.

Some data science methods have resulted into the detection of where anchorages, berths etc could be found. Such information is added without ‘user validated’ flag set, and is supposed to be validated by context mapping team before actual use.

In the Summer of 2023, Teqplay bought a relevant data set from [WorldOfPorts](https://www.worldofports.com/), holding mostly port, terminal and berth data. There is a lot of data available, including information on what services are provided in a port, what services are allowed alongside at a terminal, what products are being handled by a terminal / berth etc. All this information is not yet incorporated into PoMa currently, but information on port names / polygons, terminals with name, area and related berths and berths with related polygon and name are included in PoMa.

## 2. Data separation

In order to keep a clean separation between data that has been entered by Teqplay and data that has been provided by external providers (like worldOfPorts), the database has been split into 3 sub-databases:

1. Teqplay data: All data that has been entered by Teqplay into the system and thus is owned and administered by Teqplay.
2. External data: All data in use from an external source (like worldofports), and thus being administered by the external source
3. Merged data: The merge between the Teqplay data and external data.

By default, users of the [UI](https://poma.teqplay.nl) will see and update the Teqplay data, a separate role can be provided to users that can see (but not edit) the external data, and a third role can be provided to the users (e.g. all applications) that will use the merged view in read-only mode.

## 3. Merge strategies

There are different merge strategies applied, but for now the idea is that all information in anchor areas, pilot areas, approach areas are merged from Teqplay data for all ports. For the ‘whitelisted’ ports the port, terminal and berth information is fully taken from Teqplay, for the non-whitelisted ports the data on ports, terminals and berths are all based on the external data.

## 4. PoMa Data Models

Within PoMa different data models are being used in order to reflect the unique characteristics of a port. In the overview below the definition of each of these data-models is specified.

### 4.1 Port:

The data entity groups all things together that are usually considered as a port where vessels come to do cargo operations and/or receive alongside services. For a port we recognize different port locations and polygons:

* *single location*: a central location in the port positioned in such a way that sailing towards the port vessels will usually head roughly towards that point
* *(Inner) area*: The area encapsulating all berths and terminals where cargo operations are being executed. When a vessel enters this area, this will lead to a port.ata event, but leaving the area does only lead to a port.atd event when no outer area or margin has been specified.
* *Outer Area*: The area encapsulating the inner area with some additional margin, used to define when a vessel leaves the port. If an outer area is available, it will be used for detecting the port.atd event. The outer area might (depending on the operations executed inside the port) cover just some extra meters, but could also encapsulate anchor areas etc.
* *End of Sea Passage*: The end of sea passage is the area encapsulating the inner area + all anchor and the mainstream pilot areas. Logic of the end of sea-passage area is to trigger a ‘vessel has arrived at (not necessarily yet in) the port’
* *Nautical Mile Area (X)*: An area indicating that the vessel is X nautical miles away from arriving in the port, by default generated as circles around the port, but user adaptable to prevent for any less straightforward routes.

Typically a port is being identified by its uuid, but can always be found via it’s unlocode, or normal port name.

Single location and inner area, outer area and End Of Seapassage Port Polygons for Port of Rotterdam

A port can contain a parent port. For example, the port of Vlaardingen does not have it’s own authority and is managed by the port of Rotterdam. Therefore the port of Rotterdam is the parent port of the port of Vlaardingen. A port can only have a maximum of one parent port.

### 4.2 Terminal

The terminal within PoMa describes an area where cargo operations can take place between vessel/ship and land. Each terminal is linked to one or more ports via the unlocodes of these ports. When a terminal is linked to multiple ports (mostly because one is the sub-port of another port like Vlaardingen is a sub-port of Rotterdam, without it’s own port authority), also the `main port` field will be set defining what is the main port this terminal is linked to.

The terminal can have a cargo category type (e.g. Dry Bulk, Wet Bulk, Container, Roro, Passenger, Breakbulk). The following location based data is available for a terminal:

* *single location*: a central location in the terminal, which defines a clear location where the terminal is, and can be used for e.g. route calculations.
* *Area:* The outer area of the terminal covers both the land and the water area covered by this terminal. The water area covers the area where the related berths are located, but no berths that are not related to this terminal.
* *Mooring area:* The area indicating the border where normally the mooring process is initiated and ended. Within this area normally the vessel is not sailing but manoeuvring to/from the berth

### 4.3 Berth

The berth typically is an area where a vessel can moor to execute cargo operations with the shore, or where a vessel can moor to wait or do ship-to-ship transfer. Typically a berth is inside a port, and one will need to pay a port-fee to the port authorities to moor at a berth.

A berth can be linked to 0 or 1 terminals, and to 0 or more ports. A berth can have the cargo category type, just like the terminal. A berth has a (typical) rectangular area in which the transponder of the vessel needs to be counted as a berth visit.

### 4.4 Anchor places

An anchor place is what is normally indicated as an anchorage, a location at sea where ships can lower anchors. In most cases, this is a location outside of the port area, and no port fees are due if you anchor, but some exceptions are now where these areas are inside the port (like Singapore, New York) or where an anchorage fee has to be paid.

An anchor area in PoMa only has a single polygon.

Anchorages for the port of Rotterdam

### 4.5 Pilot Boarding places

A Pilot boarding place is a Teqplay concept about the place where typically the pilot goes on board the sea vessel on arrival or disembarks from the sea-vessel on departure. Such areas are defined on a nautical map by a pilot buoy with a single location.

The pilot boarding place can be specific for specific vessels. In Rotterdam, a pilot boarding place exits for:

* All deep-draught vessels
* LNG vessels
* The other vessels

### 4.6 Approach areas

An approach area in Poma is an area in which a vessel trespasses before or while sailing into the port, and that marks a relevant moment to be notified about. Typically such an area marks a point-of-no return to indicate the vessel will come to the terminal. There can be 0 or more approach areas per port.

### 4.7 Breakwater areas [deprecated]

Breakwater areas were originally those areas where salt water from the sea and sweet water from inland meet each other. Originally breakwater areas were used as a kind of approach areas, but since all breakwater areas will be also approach areas, there is not much need to distinguish them from approach areas. Therefore breakwater areas are discontinued.

### 4.8 Locks

A lock is a device used for raising and lowering boats, ships, and other watercraft between stretches of water of different levels on river and canal waterways. Within PoMa the lock is represented with a name and an area covering the chamber(s) of the lock. This way it can be monitored when vessels enter and leave locks.

### 4.9 Approach routes

An approach route is a typical route a vessel is sailing when approaching a certain port. Approach routes are used to help understand the route a vessel will sail from its current position to the port entrance. The specific approach route can be used to make accurate predictions on when a vessel will arrive in the port. Approach routes exist of linestrings, no areas.

It is expected that approach routes (which cover only the last day of sailing before entering the port) will be taken over by RouteScout completely.

### 4.10 Custom Areas

Any other areas that need to be monitored, but do not comply with any of the definitions above, can still be added as a custom area. Each custom area can be standalone without any relation to a port, but can also be linked to one or multiple ports.
